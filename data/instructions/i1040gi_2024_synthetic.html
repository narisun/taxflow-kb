2024 Instructions for Form 1040 and 1040-SR

Department of the Treasury — Internal Revenue Service | Cat. No. 24811V

What's New

Standard deduction increased.

For 2024, the standard deduction amounts have increased. For single filers and married filing separately, the standard deduction is $14,600. For married filing jointly and qualifying surviving spouse, the standard deduction is $29,200. For head of household, the standard deduction is $21,900. See Line 12 for more information.

Retirement plan contribution limits increased.

The limit on elective deferrals to 401(k), 403(b), and most 457 plans increased to $23,000 for 2024. The catch-up contribution limit for taxpayers age 50 or older increased to $30,500. See Publication 590-A for details. Also see Lines 1a through 1h.

HSA contribution limits increased.

For 2024, if you have self-only HDHP coverage, your HSA contribution limit is $4,150. For family HDHP coverage, the limit is $8,300. See Schedule 1, Line 13 and Publication 969.

SALT deduction cap extended.

The $10,000 ($5,000 for married filing separately) cap on state and local tax deductions continues to apply for 2024. See Schedule A instructions and Line 12.

Reminders

IRS.gov information.

Go to IRS.gov for the latest information about Form 1040 and its instructions, such as legislation enacted after they were published. Form 1040 has been redesigned. Form 1040-SR, U.S. Tax Return for Seniors, is available for taxpayers who are 65 or older.

Electronic filing identification numbers (EFIN).

If you use a paid tax professional to electronically file your return, the preparer must have a valid 6-digit Electronic Filing Identification Number (EFIN) assigned by the IRS. See Publication 3112 for EFIN requirements. The EFIN must be exactly 6 numeric digits. See Paid Preparer Use Only section.

Social security numbers (SSN).

You must enter your SSN (and your spouse's SSN if filing jointly) in the spaces provided on Form 1040. You can't file a paper return without entering SSNs. If you or your spouse were issued a social security number but have since obtained an individual taxpayer identification number (ITIN), enter the ITIN instead. See Name, Address, and SSN.

Filing Information

Tax year.

Use the 2024 Form 1040 to file your 2024 federal income tax return. The tax year code on your return must be 2024. Returns filed with an incorrect tax year code will be rejected. If you are filing a return for a prior year, use the form for that year. Do not use this 2024 form for a tax year other than 2024. See Form 1040, Page 1, Tax Year.

Filing Status.

Check only one box on line 1 of Form 1040 to indicate your filing status. Valid filing status codes are: 1 (Single), 2 (Married Filing Jointly), 3 (Married Filing Separately), 4 (Head of Household), and 5 (Qualifying Surviving Spouse). Your filing status determines your standard deduction, tax bracket, and eligibility for certain credits and deductions. Your filing status also affects the SALT deduction cap. See SALT cap and Line 12.

Name, Address, and Social Security Number.

Print or type the information in the spaces provided. Enter your primary social security number in the "Your SSN" box. If filing jointly, enter your spouse's SSN in the "Spouse's SSN" box. Both SSNs must be valid numbers issued by the Social Security Administration. A missing or invalid primary SSN will result in rejection of the return. See also Reminder — SSNs.

Line Instructions for Form 1040

Lines 1a–1h. Total Wages, Salaries, Tips, and Other Compensation.

Enter your total wages, salaries, tips, and other compensation on Line 1a. This is the total from all Forms W-2 you received. If you are a statutory employee (box 13 of Form W-2 checked), do not include those wages here; instead report them on Schedule C. If you received a Form W-2c (corrected), use the corrected amounts.

Lines 1b through 1h capture specific components of compensation including household employee wages (1b), tip income not reported to your employer (1c), Medicaid waiver payments (1d), taxable dependent care benefits (1e), employer adoption benefits (1f), wages from Form 8919 (1g), and other earned income (1h). The total of Lines 1a through 1h flows to Line 1z.

The wages you report on Lines 1a–1h should reconcile with the total wages shown on all attached Forms W-2. See also Schedule C for self-employment income. For 401(k) contributions, see What's New — Retirement plan limits and Publication 590-A.

Lines 2a–2b. Interest.

Line 2a — Tax-exempt interest. Enter the total of all tax-exempt interest you received or that was credited to your account during the year. Although this interest is not taxable, you must report it because it may affect the taxability of your Social Security benefits and other tax computations.

Line 2b — Taxable interest. Enter the total of all taxable interest income. If the total is more than $1,500, you must also attach Schedule B (Form 1040). Taxable interest includes interest from bank accounts, money market funds, and U.S. obligations (unless excluded). See Schedule B instructions and Publication 550. See Schedule B attachment requirement.

Schedule B Attachment — Interest and Dividends.

You must attach Schedule B (Form 1040) if your total taxable interest income (Line 2b) or ordinary dividends (Line 3b) exceeds $1,500, or if you received interest or dividends as a nominee for another person, or if you had a foreign account or received a distribution from a foreign trust. Failure to attach Schedule B when required will result in a reject error. See Lines 2a–2b and Lines 3a–3b.

Lines 3a–3b. Dividends.

Line 3a — Qualified dividends. Enter qualified dividends from box 1b of all Forms 1099-DIV. Qualified dividends are generally taxed at lower capital gain rates. Qualified dividends are a subset of the total ordinary dividends reported on Line 3b and must not be entered separately on Line 3b. Do not include distributions from money market funds (these are reported as interest), or dividends paid by a foreign corporation unless the stock meets the holding period requirement.

Line 3b — Ordinary dividends. Enter the total ordinary dividends from box 1a of all Forms 1099-DIV. This amount includes the qualified dividends from Line 3a. If the total exceeds $1,500, attach Schedule B. See also Schedule B Attachment and Publication 550.

Lines 4a–4b. IRA Distributions.

Line 4a — Total amount. Enter the gross distribution you received from all traditional IRAs, SEP IRAs, and SIMPLE IRAs during 2024. This is the amount from box 1 of Form 1099-R. Do not include Roth IRA distributions here (see Line 5a–5b if these are pension or annuity distributions).

Line 4b — Taxable amount. Enter only the taxable portion of your IRA distributions. If your IRA contributions were fully deductible, the full distribution is generally taxable. If you made nondeductible contributions, use Form 8606 to figure the taxable amount. An early distribution before age 59½ may be subject to a 10% additional tax on Form 5329. See Publication 590-B for more detail.

Lines 5a–5b. Pensions and Annuities.

Line 5a — Total pension and annuity income. Enter the gross pension and annuity distributions from box 1 of Form 1099-R. Include distributions from profit-sharing plans, retirement plans, and commercial annuities.

Line 5b — Taxable amount. Enter the taxable amount of pension and annuity income. If box 2a of Form 1099-R shows the taxable amount, use that figure. Otherwise use the General Rule or Simplified Method to figure the taxable amount. See Publication 939 and Form 1040 instructions Appendix B.

Lines 6a–6b. Social Security Benefits.

Line 6a — Total Social Security benefits. Enter the total benefits you received from the Social Security Administration as shown in box 5 of Form SSA-1099. If you received railroad retirement benefits treated as Social Security, include them here using Form RRB-1099.

Line 6b — Taxable Social Security benefits. Up to 85% of your Social Security benefits may be taxable depending on your provisional income. Use the Social Security Benefits Worksheet in these instructions to determine the taxable amount. If you file Form 2555 or Form 4563, use Publication 915 instead. Modified AGI affects the taxability calculation; see Line 11 — AGI.

Line 7. Capital Gain or (Loss).

If you have capital gains or losses to report, you must attach Schedule D (Form 1040) unless you received Form 1099-DIV reporting only capital gain distributions (box 2a) with no other capital gains or losses. Enter the net capital gain or loss from Schedule D, Line 21 (or Line 16 if you are not required to file Schedule D). Net capital losses are limited to ($3,000) per year (($1,500) for married filing separately). See Schedule D instructions and Publication 544. See also Schedule D requirement.

Schedule D Attachment.

Attach Schedule D (Form 1040) when you have capital gains or losses from asset sales or exchanges, capital loss carryovers from prior years, or when you received Schedule K-1 amounts with capital gain or loss. When the condition requiring Schedule D is met and you do not attach it, the return will be rejected. See Line 7 for capital gain reporting.

Line 8. Other Income.

Enter the amount from Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Line 10. Schedule 1 captures additional income sources not covered by Lines 1 through 7, including self-employment income (Schedule C), rental income (Schedule E), alimony received (for pre-2019 divorce decrees), gambling winnings, cancellation of debt, prizes and awards, and other miscellaneous income. See Schedule 1 instructions for a complete list of additional income items.

If you have self-employment income, you must also attach Schedule C and Schedule SE. See Schedule C requirement and Schedule E requirement.

Schedule C Requirement — Business Income.

Attach Schedule C (Form 1040) if you had profit or loss from a business or profession you operated as a sole proprietor, or if you were a statutory employee with certain income. Your net profit or loss from Schedule C carries to Schedule 1, Line 3, and flows into Line 8 of Form 1040. When self-employment net profit is nonzero, Schedule C must be attached or the return will be rejected. See also Schedule SE for self-employment tax. See Line 8.

Schedule E Requirement — Supplemental Income.

Attach Schedule E (Form 1040) when you have rental income or loss, royalties, partnership income, S corporation income, estate or trust income, or certain other supplemental income. When supplemental income or loss amounts are nonzero, Schedule E must be attached or the return will be rejected. See Line 8.

Line 9. Total Income.

Add Lines 1z, 2b, 3b, 4b, 5b, 6b, 7, and 8. Enter the result here. Total income is the starting point for computing adjusted gross income. If the result is a loss, enter it as a negative number. Note that Line 3a (qualified dividends) is a component already included in Line 3b and must not be added again. Capital gains from Line 7 are already net of capital losses carried from Schedule D.

Line 10. Adjustments to Income.

Enter the amount from Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Line 26. Schedule 1 captures above-the-line deductions (adjustments) that reduce your income before computing AGI. These include educator expenses, student loan interest, tuition and fees deduction, health savings account (HSA) deductions, self-employed health insurance, SEP and SIMPLE plan contributions, self-employment tax deduction, and alimony paid under a pre-2019 divorce decree.

HSA Deduction — Schedule 1, Line 13.

If you made contributions to a Health Savings Account (HSA) for 2024, complete Form 8889 and attach it to your return. The deductible contribution amount from Form 8889, Line 13, flows to Schedule 1, Line 13. The 2024 HSA contribution limits are $4,150 for self-only HDHP coverage and $8,300 for family HDHP coverage. Additional catch-up contributions of $1,000 are allowed for taxpayers age 55 or older. You must have been enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP) to contribute. See Publication 969 and Form 8889 instructions. See What's New — HSA limits.

Student Loan Interest Deduction — Schedule 1, Line 21.

You may be able to deduct up to $2,500 of student loan interest paid in 2024. The deduction phases out for married filing jointly filers with modified AGI between $155,000 and $185,000 (between $75,000 and $90,000 for all other filers). You cannot claim this deduction if your filing status is married filing separately. The deductible amount is computed on the Student Loan Interest Deduction Worksheet. See Publication 970. See Line 11 — Modified AGI for how MAGI affects this deduction.

Line 11. Adjusted Gross Income (AGI).

Subtract Line 10 from Line 9. The result is your adjusted gross income. AGI is one of the most important numbers on your return — it determines your eligibility for many deductions and credits, and is used to compute modified AGI (MAGI) for specific phase-out calculations. If Line 10 exceeds Line 9, enter zero; you cannot have a negative AGI for tax computation purposes.

Your AGI must equal Total Income (Line 9) minus the above-the-line deductions on Schedule 1, Line 26. This relationship is verified by the IRS e-file validation system. Discrepancies between Line 9, Line 10, and Line 11 will result in a math error reject. See also Line 9 and Line 10.

Line 12. Standard Deduction or Itemized Deductions.

Enter either your standard deduction or the amount from Schedule A, whichever is larger (or whichever you choose, if you prefer to itemize even if smaller). You cannot take both a standard deduction and itemized deductions. If you itemize, attach Schedule A and enter the amount from Schedule A, Line 17.

The 2024 standard deduction amounts by filing status:

Filing StatusStandard Deduction
Single / Married Filing Separately$14,600
Married Filing Jointly / Qualifying Surviving Spouse$29,200
Head of Household$21,900

If you can be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return, your standard deduction is limited. See the Standard Deduction Worksheet for Dependents. Blind taxpayers or those age 65 or older receive an additional standard deduction amount. See Publication 501.

If you itemize and claim state and local taxes on Schedule A, Line 5, the total state and local tax (SALT) deduction is limited to $10,000 ($5,000 for married filing separately). See Schedule A — SALT Cap and What's New — SALT.

Schedule A — State and Local Tax (SALT) Deduction Cap.

If you itemize deductions on Schedule A, the total deduction for state and local income taxes (or general sales taxes) plus real estate taxes and personal property taxes is capped at $10,000 per return ($5,000 for married filing separately). This cap applies regardless of your filing status or income level. Amounts claimed on Schedule A, Line 5, that exceed the applicable cap will trigger a reject error. Attach Schedule A when itemizing deductions.

Line 13. Qualified Business Income (QBI) Deduction.

If you have qualified business income from a sole proprietorship, partnership, S corporation, or trust, you may be entitled to a deduction of up to 20% of your qualified business income. Complete Form 8995 or Form 8995-A and enter the allowable deduction here. The QBI deduction is subject to limitations based on your taxable income and the type of business. See Form 8995 instructions and Publication 535 for details. This deduction does not reduce your AGI.

Line 15. Taxable Income.

Subtract Lines 12 and 13 from Line 11. If the result is zero or less, enter -0- (do not enter a negative amount). Taxable income is the base on which your federal income tax is computed. Taxable income = AGI – (Standard or Itemized Deduction) – QBI Deduction.

Line 16. Tax.

Figure your tax on the amount on Line 15 using the Tax Table (if taxable income is under $100,000) or the Tax Computation Worksheet (if $100,000 or more). Your filing status determines which column of the Tax Table or worksheet to use. If you have qualified dividends or capital gains, use the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet. Include any tax from Forms 8814 (children's investment income) or 4972 (lump-sum distributions).

Line 17. Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT).

Enter the amount from Form 6251, Line 11, if you owe the alternative minimum tax. Complete Form 6251 to determine if you owe AMT. The AMT applies if your tentative minimum tax exceeds your regular tax. The AMT exemption amounts for 2024 are $85,700 for single filers and $133,300 for married filing jointly, with phase-outs above certain income thresholds. See Form 6251 instructions and Publication 17.

Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) — Form 8960.

The 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax applies to the lesser of your net investment income or the excess of your modified adjusted gross income over the applicable threshold. For 2024, the threshold is $200,000 (single), $250,000 (married filing jointly), $125,000 (married filing separately), or $200,000 (head of household). If you owe NIIT, complete Form 8960 and attach it to your return. Enter the amount from Form 8960, Line 21, on Schedule 2, Line 12. See Form 8960 requirement.

Form 8960 — Net Investment Income Tax Attachment.

You must attach Form 8960 whenever you have net investment income tax on Line 17 of Schedule 2 (carried to Form 1040, Line 17). Net investment income includes dividends, interest, capital gains, rental income, royalties, and passive activity income. A return claiming NIIT without an attached Form 8960 will be rejected. The NIIT computation is: 3.8% × MIN(Net Investment Income, Excess of MAGI over threshold). See Line 17 — NIIT and Form 8960 instructions.

Line 19. Child Tax Credit / Credit for Other Dependents.

Enter the amount from Schedule 8812 (Form 1040). The child tax credit is $2,000 per qualifying child under age 17. The credit for other dependents is $500. Both credits begin to phase out when your modified AGI exceeds $200,000 ($400,000 for married filing jointly). Use Schedule 8812 to compute the allowable credit and any additional child tax credit. Complete and attach Form 8812 if claiming these credits. See Form 8812 instructions.

Line 24. Total Tax.

Add Lines 16, 17, and 18. This is your total federal income tax liability before applying payments and credits from Lines 25 through 32. The total tax must equal the sum of all components including income tax, AMT, NIIT, additional Medicare tax, self-employment tax, and other taxes from Schedule 2.

Line 25a. Federal Income Tax Withheld.

Enter the total federal income tax withheld as shown on your Forms W-2 (box 2) and Forms 1099. Combine all withholding from wages, retirement distributions, gambling winnings, pensions, and other income. Attach all Forms W-2 and Forms 1099 that show federal income tax withheld. Withholding shown on a Form W-2G (gambling winnings) is entered here. Do not include state income tax withheld; that goes on Schedule A if you itemize.

Line 26. 2024 Estimated Tax Payments.

Enter the total of your quarterly estimated tax payments made for 2024, including any overpayment from 2023 that you elected to apply to 2024. The due dates for 2024 estimated tax payments were April 15, June 17, and September 16, 2024, and January 15, 2025. Use Form 1040-ES to make estimated payments. See Publication 505 for more information on estimated tax.

Line 27. Earned Income Credit (EIC).

You may qualify for the earned income credit (EIC) if you have earned income below a certain amount and meet other requirements. The amount of EIC depends on your income, filing status, and the number of qualifying children. Enter the amount from the EIC Worksheet or use the EIC Table in Publication 596. You cannot claim EIC if your investment income exceeds $11,600 for 2024. If you have a qualifying child, complete Schedule EIC. The maximum EIC for 2024 is $7,830 for three or more qualifying children. See Publication 596 and the EIC instructions for eligibility requirements.

Line 28. Additional Child Tax Credit.

Enter the amount from Schedule 8812, Line 15c. This refundable credit may reduce your tax below zero. If you have a qualifying child and the child tax credit (Line 19) exceeds your tax liability, you may be entitled to the additional child tax credit. Complete Schedule 8812 to determine the amount. See also Form 8812 instructions.

Line 33. Total Payments.

Add Lines 25a through 32. This is the total of all your federal income tax payments, including withholding and refundable credits. Compare this total to your total tax on Line 24 to determine whether you have a refund or owe additional tax.

Line 35a. Refund.

If Line 33 is more than Line 24, subtract Line 24 from Line 33. This is the amount you overpaid. You can have all or part of your refund directly deposited into your bank account — enter your routing number and account number on Lines 35b through 35d. You can also apply all or part of your refund to your 2025 estimated tax — see Line 36. Refund processing generally takes 21 days for e-filed returns and 6 to 8 weeks for paper returns.

Lines 36–37. Amount Applied to Next Year and Amount You Owe.

Line 36 — If you want to apply part or all of your refund to your 2025 estimated taxes, enter that amount on Line 36. This amount reduces your Line 35a refund. Line 37 — If Line 24 is more than Line 33, subtract Line 33 from Line 24. This is the amount you owe. Pay by April 15, 2025, to avoid interest and penalties. You can pay online at IRS.gov/payments, by check payable to the U.S. Treasury, or by electronic funds withdrawal when e-filing. See Publication 17 for payment options.

Paid Preparer Use Only

Paid Preparer Information.

If you paid someone to prepare your return, that person must sign and complete the "Paid Preparer Use Only" section. The preparer must provide their Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN), their firm's name, address, and EIN, and their phone number. If you are filing electronically, the preparer must have a valid 6-digit Electronic Filing Identification Number (EFIN) issued by the IRS. The EFIN must match the IRS EFIN database for the return to be accepted. See Reminder — EFIN.

Signature

Sign Your Return.

Your return is not complete without your signature. If you are filing jointly, both spouses must sign. The signature date should reflect the date you signed the return. Under penalties of perjury, you declare that the return is true, correct, and complete to the best of your knowledge. For e-filed returns, you provide your signature through your self-selected Personal Identification Number (PIN). Both spouses must provide their PIN if filing jointly. An unsigned return will be rejected. Enter the signature date in the format required by the e-file system.
